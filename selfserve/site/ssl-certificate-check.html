HTTPS and SSL certificate check for your website

See whether your site loads over HTTPS and sends the security headers browsers look for. The full report also opens a direct connection to read your certificate's expiry date.

Only check websites you own or are allowed to check. The free check makes three read-only requests to the site (the homepage, robots.txt and sitemap.xml) and public DNS lookups for your SPF and DMARC email records.

What it checks

  • HTTPS — whether your homepage loads over a verified HTTPS connection. Browsers mark sites without it as not secure.
  • HSTS — the Strict-Transport-Security header, which tells browsers to always use HTTPS for your site, with a max-age of at least 6 months.
  • Security headers — Content-Security-Policy, X-Content-Type-Options, X-Frame-Options (or CSP frame-ancestors) and Referrer-Policy.
  • Certificate expiry (full report) — the certificate's expiry date from a direct TLS connection, judged against how long the certificate lasts, so a short-lived certificate that renews itself isn't flagged.
  • Private files (full report) — whether /.env or /.git/HEAD can be fetched from your site. The checker asks for each once and never downloads them.

The free check confirms HTTPS and reads the headers from your homepage. It doesn't inspect the certificate itself; that is in the full report, and the free check says so instead of guessing.

What it doesn't do

  • It isn't a penetration test or a vulnerability scan, and it doesn't try to break in.
  • It doesn't check every page or every subdomain, only the address you type.
  • It doesn't grade your server's TLS versions or cipher settings.

Questions

My certificate shows only a few weeks left. Is that bad?
Not if it renews itself. Many certificates last 90 days and renew automatically. The full report compares the time left with the certificate's own lifetime and only flags one that looks close to expiring without renewing.
Why does the free check say the certificate wasn't inspected?
The free check makes only three requests, so it confirms HTTPS but doesn't open the separate connection needed to read the certificate. The full report does.
Can I fix these myself?
HTTPS and certificates are usually switched on at your web host. Security headers are often a setting there too. Each finding in the full report says who usually makes the change and roughly how long it takes.
Does it change my site?
No. Every request is read-only.
