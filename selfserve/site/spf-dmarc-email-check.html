SPF and DMARC check for your business email

Type your website and see whether your domain publishes SPF and DMARC records. These public DNS records tell other mail servers who may send email as you, and what to do with email that pretends to be you.

Only check websites you own or are allowed to check. The free check makes three read-only requests to the site (the homepage, robots.txt and sitemap.xml) and public DNS lookups for your SPF and DMARC email records.

What it checks

  • SPF — a v=spf1 TXT record on your domain, whether there is more than one (only one is allowed), and whether it ends in +all, which lets anyone send as you, or ?all, which leaves unlisted senders neutral.
  • DMARC — a v=DMARC1 record at _dmarc on your domain, and its policy. A policy of none only monitors; quarantine or reject actually protects you.
  • DKIM (full report) — looks up common DKIM selector names (such as google, default, selector1 and selector2).

The domain comes from your website address (www. is dropped). For a subdomain, the parent domain's records are checked too. Both SPF and DMARC are part of the free check, because they are public DNS lookups that never touch your site.

What the results can and can't say

  • DKIM keys sit under a name only your email provider knows. If none of the common names answers, the report says not measured, not that DKIM is missing.
  • If a DNS lookup doesn't finish in time, that check is listed as not measured, never as a fail.
  • It doesn't send any email or test whether your messages reach inboxes.
  • If your business email uses a different domain from your website, the records checked are for the website's domain.

Questions

What is SPF?
A public DNS record that lists which servers may send email for your domain. Without it, scam email can pose as you and your real email is more likely to land in spam.
What is DMARC?
A public DNS record that tells inboxes what to do with email that fails the SPF or DKIM checks: nothing (none), send it to spam (quarantine) or block it (reject).
Where do I add these records?
At whoever runs your domain's DNS, usually your domain registrar or web host. Your email provider gives you the exact values. The full report explains each fix.
Do you read my email?
No. The check only reads public DNS records that anyone can look up.
